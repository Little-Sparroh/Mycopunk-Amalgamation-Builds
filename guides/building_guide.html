Building Guide

How to put together a Mycopunk loadout: element first, then weapons and ammo, then grenade, employee, and heavy. Ranked kill-time builds live on the build pages — this guide is the method.

Start here: loadout checklist

  1. Element — pick the damage type that drives the rest of the kit (and any multi-element plan).
  2. Weapon pair + ammo loop — two guns that feed each other; know how magazine/reserve regen works.
  3. Grenade — usually match weapon element; Photon or Snowball when you mean to break that rule.
  4. Employee — mobility fantasy; preference most of the time (see exceptions under Employees).
  5. Heavy — only if it earns the slot. Default strong pick: Laser Cannon. Never Last Argument.

Details below. Worked examples at the end; cheat sheet in Quick reference.

1. Loadout anatomy

SlotRole
Weapon A / Weapon BMain damage and the ammo economy between them
GrenadeBurst damage, healing, mobility, utility, or flex element
EmployeeHow you move (and rare weapon synergies)
Heavy weaponTool / support in the fourth slot — not a second primary plan

Everything below is how to fill these slots on purpose.

2. Elements

Choose weapon element first. Grenade and a lot of upgrade value follow from that choice. Most weapons start as Normal before element upgrades.

Core elements

ElementWhat it doesBuild note
NormalBasic damage, no extra effectsGenerally the weaker plan unless Photon or a specific gun wants it
FireDoT that ticks for 160Default strong clear/pressure identity
ShockDoT that ticks for 160Pairs with Soak; grenade choice is more situational
AcidDoT that ticks for 160Same tick ballpark as Fire/Shock; often the last trinity pick

Fire, Shock, and Acid share a 160 tick value but are not interchangeable: grenades, weapon kits, and tech elements (Soak, etc.) pull them apart.

Conditional / tech elements

ElementWhat it doesBuild note
Decay+20% damage to all other damage typesAmplifier — build around feeding other elements, not solo clear

2.1 Weapon element

Each weapon offers an element path. That choice has knock-on effects for:

  • which grenade is natural

Lock element before you obsess over a second gun.

2.2 Grenade pairing

Rule: grenade element follows weapon element.

Exception — Photon Disc: can assume any of the main trinity or Normal, and can change element mid-mission via upgrades. That keeps more weapon pairs legal when elements don’t match.

Exception — Snowball: only when you are actually playing Cryo.

3. Two-weapon loadouts

Two weapons that play well together matter as much as either gun alone. The game’s ammo rules are why.

3.1 Primary + secondary

Assume you want near-100% uptime on the first weapon and will rarely swap. This is the dominant late style once you have the tools — strong secondaries exist mainly to feed ammo and cover jobs the main gun is bad at (e.g. bosses).

Go-to secondaries: Plate Launcher, Swarm Launcher, Shocklance. Globbler is an honorable mention.

3.2 Dual primary

Two “main” guns, swapping for DPS. This is how most players spend early through mid game until secondaries and passive ammo tools come online. There is no inherent downside if you can keep both guns fed and DPS high.

StylePlay patternWhen it shines
Primary + secondaryHold main; secondary is economy / coverageOnce Plate/Swarm/Shocklance-style tools and passives exist
Dual primarySwap for damage; both guns earn timeEarly game, or any pair that both deserve trigger time

3.3 Ammo generation

Rule: damage with one weapon fills the magazine, then the reserves, of the other weapon. Efficiency is pair-dependent — some combos top each other off easily; others feel starved.

If both guns run dry together, the pair or the passives are wrong. But the game will refill your ammo if you hit 0 in both.

Passive ammo generation

Goal: generate ammo for the stowed gun while you hold something else.

  1. DoT carryover — if a weapon applies damage over time, that damage keeps filling the other weapon even after you swap. Longer DoTs (e.g. Bees) are better engines.
  2. Off-hand damage upgrades — effects that deal damage while the weapon is not held:
    • Plate Launcher — Fencing Construction
    • Swarm Launcher — Crop Duster
    • Photon Disc — Bullet Time (same goal, different behavior from the launcher passives — don’t treat it as identical)

Primary + secondary builds lean hard on these so the main gun never leaves your hands.

4. Grenades

GrenadeRoleTake whenSkip when

Rough pick order for most builds: Incendiary → Voltaic → Photon → Caustic → Snowball.

5. Employees and mobility

All employees are viable. Preference most of the time — pick the movement fantasy you like. A few weapons still bias the choice (below).

EmployeeMobilityNotes
WranglerBest 3D movement in the gameFast and very hard to kill
BruiserStrong arcs if you leap-cancelAlt route: Modular Construction + Backboost for high-skill movement
ScrapperClean destination control (slower than Wrangler in pure 3D)Allies can share the movement — best vertical “put me there” kit
GliderSolid horizontal speedLowest skill floor; least versatile vertically / in full 3D
MycologistArc movement like Bruiser, but launch at end of arcGenerally better arcs than Bruiser’s cancel-dependent line; highest complexity overall

Not pure preference

  • Accelerator damage scaling favors Wrangler
  • Carver gets the most out of Scrapper
  • Gunship Cannon + Gunship upgrade is a poor fit on Bruiser

6. Heavy weapons

Heavies are tools, not your main damage plan — but the slot still has a clear ranking.

HeavyVerdict
Laser CannonBest heavy in the game — take this every time for horde clear or boss rushing
Street SweeperSupport weapon; swap mechanics that boost the rest of the loadout
Beam RipperPierce damage; not worthless, not great
Last ArgumentNever — meme weapon

Default: Laser Cannon. Most times you won't have a heavy on hand and that is fine.

7. Example loadouts

Employees are preference most of the time (see exceptions above). Heavy defaults to Laser Cannon unless you know you want Street Sweeper support tech.

DMLR + Plate Launcher + Voltaic

  • Fantasy: Simple execution once upgrades are in place — infinite laser charge.
  • Weapons: DMLR (main) + Plate Launcher (secondary / ammo engine).
  • Ammo loop: None! Your battery will charge with the Voltaic Grenade's Livewire.
  • Grenade: Voltaic — mobility while the gun does the work.
  • Employee: Preference.
  • Heavy: Laser Cannon (default).
  • Why it works: Classic primary + secondary economy; Plate exists to keep the laser damage up.
  • Watch out for: Needs the upgrade package online before it feels “infinite.”

Gunship Cannon + Plate Launcher + Incendiary

  • Fantasy: Classic blow-everything-up kit that just works.
  • Weapons: Gunship Cannon + Plate Launcher.
  • Ammo loop: Plate passive feeding Gunship uptime.
  • Grenade: Incendiary — default heal + damage package.
  • Employee: Preference — avoid Bruiser if you run Gunship upgrade with Gunship Cannon.
  • Heavy: Laser Cannon (default).
  • Why it works: Straightforward primary + secondary; Incendiary matches “delete pack” energy.
  • Watch out for: Employee mismatch with Gunship+Gunship upgrade on Bruiser.

Trident + Swarm Launcher + Photon Disc

  • Fantasy: Low-threat simplicity — Trident kills most things; Swarm blows up what you mark.
  • Weapons: Trident + Swarm Launcher.
  • Ammo loop: Swarm Crop Duster + DoTs; Photon Bullet Time as extra passive pressure/flex.
  • Grenade: Photon Disc — flex element (trinity or Normal) so pairing stays legal.
  • Employee: Preference.
  • Heavy: Laser Cannon (default).
  • Why it works: Easy roles; Photon forgives element friction.
  • Watch out for: Photon is doing kit-enable work but can be swapped for Incendiary at nearly no cost.

Shocklance + Plate Launcher + Incendiary

  • Fantasy: Shocklance clears the map; Plate supports when bosses show up.
  • Weapons: Shocklance + Plate Launcher.
  • Ammo loop: Plate Fencing Construction while Shocklance does map work; swap to Plate for boss pressure.
  • Grenade: Incendiary — default strongest overall package.
  • Employee: Preference.
  • Heavy: Laser Cannon (default).
  • Why it works: Still-kicking clear + boss secondary split; textbook primary/secondary jobs.
  • Watch out for: Treat Plate as the boss plan, not a second clear toy.

For full ranked lists and kill times, use the build pages (Mid Game / No Restriction / etc.) and the weapon tierlist.

8. Quick reference

Checklist: Element → weapon pair + ammo loop → grenade → employee → heavy (Laser Cannon or skip).

Ammo rule: Damage on A fills mag then reserves of B (pair-dependent). Passives: DoTs while swapped; Fencing Construction (Plate), Crop Duster (Swarm), Bullet Time (Photon — different implementation).

Elements (numbers): Fire / Shock / Acid DoT tick 160; Bees = longer DoT; Cryo shatter 200; Decay +20% other types; Rot +30% vs metallic; Soak = Shock apply + Shock amp.

Grenades: Incendiary (default) · Voltaic (speed) · Photon (flex/mid-mission element) · Caustic (utility, low pick) · Snowball (Cryo, niche).

Secondaries to know: Plate Launcher · Swarm Launcher · Shocklance · (Globbler HM).

Heavies: Laser Cannon ≫ Street Sweeper > Beam Ripper ≫ Last Argument (never).

Employees: Wrangler best 3D · Scrapper best precise/team vertical · Glider easiest horizontal · Bruiser leap-cancel or Modular+Backboost · Mycologist end-of-arc launch. Exceptions: Accelerator→Wrangler, Carver→Scrapper, Gunship+Gunship upgrade≠Bruiser.
